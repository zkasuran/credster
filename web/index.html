Every AI credit, assayed.

Credster is a market for spare AI inference credits. Every seller is graded before it can trade: is it the model it claims, and is the token count honest? The grade lives on chain. An F suspends the listing.

§01 · The problem

Resold credits hide two risks you can’t see.

People hold AI credits they don’t use: a team plan, a NEAR AI Cloud balance, spare capacity on their own GPU. Agents need inference and would happily buy it below list price. Reselling already happens. The buyer just can’t check what it got.

"model": "Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B"

Risk 01

Model substitution

The endpoint says Qwen3.8-27B and serves a cheaper model, or an uncensored (“abliterated”) copy of the same one. The model field in the response is whatever the seller writes there.

used70
billed95

Risk 02

Inflated metering

The endpoint serves the right model and reports 35% more tokens than it used. Every call costs a little more than it should, and the buyer has no count of its own to compare.

Escrow and spending limits don’t help here. The buyer pays exactly what was agreed, for something other than what was agreed.

§02 · How a grade is made

The grader buys like a customer, then checks the goods.

Before a listing can trade, the grader places a small trial order on it and probes the endpoint with seven calls, signed with the trial order’s key like any buyer’s. Three checks decide the grade.

01The touchstone: a streak compared with reference needles.

Check 01

Token-count fingerprint

Three fixed probe texts, dense with mixed scripts, emoji and code. The prompt token count depends on the model’s tokenizer and chat template, so the three counts work as a fingerprint. Measured against NEAR AI Cloud directly, they were identical on repeat calls and differed between model families.

A mismatch means a different model. Counts that are all the reference times one factor mean inflated billing.

Check 02

TEE signature

For models NEAR AI Cloud serves inside a TEE, the seller node relays the provider’s signature for one probe. The grader rebuilds the text that should have been signed, recovers the signer and looks it up in NEAR AI’s public attestation report for the claimed model.

A valid signature that names a different model is proof of substitution. It caught the sibling seller, whose abliterated Qwen has the same tokenizer and passed the fingerprint.

The grade goes on chain

§03 · Four marks

Three of them can trade.

A listing can be bought only while its grade is passing and fresh. A new endpoint clears the grade until the grader probes it again.

  1. Grade A: TEE-signed

    Fingerprint and metering match, and a TEE signature proves which model answered. At least half the tasks right, at most 10% errors.

    Trades

  2. Grade B: No TEE for this model

    Fingerprint and metering match, and the model has no TEE signatures at its provider to check. Same task and error bar as an A.

    Trades

  3. Grade C: Capped

    Fingerprint and metering match, but the seller withheld a TEE signature its model supports, got fewer than half the tasks right, or failed more than 10% of calls.

    Trades

  4. Grade F: Suspended

    Wrong tokenizer, scaled token counts, a TEE signature naming another model, or most calls failing. Buying is refused and the probe calls go unpaid.

    Can’t trade

§05 · Trading

Three ways to buy. Rules set every price.

Buy at the ask

The deposit is the budget. The price is the ask at the moment of the fill, and the order keeps it.

Alice bought a 0.1 NEAR order on listing #6 at the ask, 0.08 / 0.30 NEAR per million input / output tokens. The seller’s pricing rule then raised the ask to 0.088 / 0.33. Her order kept the old price.

order 14 txreprice tx

Let agents negotiate

The buyer escrows a deposit with an offer. Buyer and seller alternate, four moves at most. The buyer agent plans a price from the grade, 90% of the ask for an A and 80% for a B. It opens lower, counters once with its reasoning, then accepts or walks away. The seller agent keeps a private floor.

An optional LLM only writes the note. Rules set every price, so text in a counterparty’s note can’t move money.

Pay as you go, under a ceiling

The buyer opens a tab with a price ceiling. Each claim is priced at the seller’s ask when the claim is made, capped at the ceiling.

Bob opened a tab on listing #10 with an output ceiling of 0.15. The first claim was priced at the ask, 0.12. After the seller raised the ask to 0.30, the next claim was priced at the ceiling, 0.15.

§07 · Under the hood

One contract holds the money and the rules.

The seller’s upstream key stays on the seller’s node; buyers never see it. Every listing, grade, order, claim, dispute and refund is a contract call with a NEP-297-style event log.

  • Contract escrow and rules

    Bonds, deposits, offers, the grade gates, claims with a challenge window, disputes, slashing and refunds. Rust, near-sdk 5.30, 24 unit tests.

  • Sub-accounts as identities

    Every seller, buyer and the grader is a sub-account of one parent, so each action traces to one agent.

  • Per-order ed25519 keys

    The buyer registers a fresh key on each order and signs gateway requests with it. A leaked key can spend only that order’s remaining budget, at that order’s price.

  • NEAR AI Cloud

    The upstream for the demo sellers, the reference for fingerprints, and the source of the TEE signatures and attestation reports the grader checks.

§09 · Known limits

What it doesn’t do yet.

  1. The grader is one trusted account.

    Its reports are public and hash-bound, and anyone can rerun a grade with grade --no-post, but the contract trusts the grader’s account. A grader in a TEE, or several graders with a quorum, is not built.

  2. A fingerprint names a tokenizer family, not a model.

    Siblings that share a tokenizer give identical counts: glm-5.3 and glm-5.3-flash, or Qwen3.8-27B and its abliterated copy. The TEE check separates them where it exists. Without it, four tasks usually won’t.

  3. A careful cheater can fake the counts.

    A seller that serves another model and rewrites usage to the claimed tokenizer’s counts passes the fingerprint. For TEE models the signature still catches it; for others it does not.

  4. A seller can withhold TEE signatures.

    For a model that has them at the provider, the grade is then capped at C rather than failed.

  5. The hardware quotes weren’t checked here.

    The grader trusts the signing address that NEAR AI’s public attestation report lists for a model. It does not verify the Intel TDX and NVIDIA quotes itself.

  6. Probes can be recognised.

    The grader uses its own account. A seller that answers the grader honestly and cheats everyone else is caught only by buyers’ metering disputes, not over substitution.

  7. Testnet NEAR, no streaming.

    Native testnet NEAR only: no stablecoin, no NEAR Intents yet. The seller node refuses stream: true.

  8. Provider terms.

    Many AI providers forbid reselling API access, and each seller answers for its own provider’s terms. Self-hosted open models are the clean case.
